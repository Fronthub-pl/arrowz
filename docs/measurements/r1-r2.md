# R1 and R2: the trap lever and the tail backbite

Measured at 1000×1000, the largest board the generator allows, on 2026-09-12
and 2026-09-13.

Two ideas taken from published puzzle generators, measured before they became
knobs. How the generator cuts a board is in [the design](../design.md).

- **R1, scoring heads by the blocking graph.** Every piece that blocks a new
  piece is older than it, so the new piece's depth in the blocking graph is
  known the moment it is cut. Ranking the candidate heads by such a score
  steers difficulty while the board is built, instead of measuring it
  afterwards. It ships as `trapBias` (`--trapbias=avoid|off|seek`).
- **R2, the tail backbite** (Mansfield, 2006). When a growing piece's tail has
  no free neighbour, the move bites one of the piece's own cells next to the
  tail and reverses the path behind it, so the tail moves and growth goes on.
  `cells[0]` is never touched, so the head, the ray, the blockers and the
  piece's place in the blocking graph stay as they were. It ships as
  `backbite` (`--backbite=0..8`), the move is `Carver.backbiteTail`.

## Words used here

| word | meaning |
|---|---|
| depth | 0 when the ray from a piece's head to the exit edge crosses no piece, else 1 + the greatest depth of its blockers |
| `D` | the greatest depth, `analyse().D` (Kahn's longest path) |
| free arrows | pieces free at the start, depth 0: `f0` × `N` |
| trap | a piece blocked by exactly one other, so it looks free: `Metrics.almost` |
| stall | the growth loop stopped before the planned length (`CarverStats.stall`) |
| `strandTrunc`, `strandLoss` | how often the leftover test cut a path short, and the cells it threw away |
| `square` | every knob at its default |
| `tunnels` | `--start=tunnels` (`headBias` 1) |
| `skeleton` | `giants` 4 |
| `edge` | `warns` 6, `anticoil` 4, `pStraight` 0.65: the most winding setting the envelope allowed at 1000×1000 when this was measured |

## Running the scripts

The scripts live in `packages/engine/scripts/`. Run them from the repository
root. They write JSON lines to `/tmp/arrowz-measure/` by default and do not
create that directory, so run `mkdir -p /tmp/arrowz-measure` first. Every
script starts with
`deno run --allow-read --allow-write packages/engine/scripts/`.

| script | arguments | what it measures |
|---|---|---|
| `measure-r1-depth.ts` | `[side] [seeds] [out] [backbite]` | depth at carve time against `analyse()` |
| `measure-r1-cost.ts` | `[side] [seeds] [stride] [out]` | a ray walk per head against a line table |
| `measure-r2-backbite.ts` | `[side] [seeds] [budgetS] [out]` | the four sets at caps 0, 2, 4 and 8 |
| `measure-r1-r2-interaction.ts` | `[side] [seeds] [budgetS] [out]` | the trap lever with the backbite, its cost, the `edge` corner |
| `measure-r1-start-shadow.ts` | `[out]` | the lever with `--start`, and the undo path |
| `measure-r1-share.ts` | `[side] [seeds] [budgetS] [out]` | eleven shares of ranked cuts |
| `measure-r1-levels.ts` | `[seeds] [budgetS] [out]` | every preset option at `avoid`, `off` and `seek` |

For example, the backbite caps:

    deno run --allow-read --allow-write packages/engine/scripts/measure-r2-backbite.ts 1000 3 300

The `edge` set no longer passes `validateParams` at 1000×1000: since the
coiling discount in `straightFloor` became 0.9 (see
[envelope leaks](envelope-leaks.md)), its floor there is 0.7. So
`measure-r2-backbite.ts` and `measure-r1-r2-interaction.ts` stop with
"outside the envelope" when they reach `edge`; the rows before it still run.

## R1: the depth is known at carve time

9 boards (three sets × 3 seeds), 83 000-86 000 pieces each, plus 6 more with
`backbite` 8.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| every blocker's id < the piece it blocks | **0 violations** in ~1.3 million pieces |
| carve-time depth == depth recomputed on the finished board | **0 mismatches** |
| max depth == `analyse().D` (Kahn) | **15/15 boards** |
| depth-0 count == `analyse().f0` × N | **15/15 boards** |
| board identical to `generate()` | fingerprint equal on every checked run |

So `absorbLeftover` and `undoLast` leave the depth of an existing piece alone,
and the recurrence reproduces Kahn's longest path exactly. It holds with the
tail backbite on too, because the move never touches `cells[0]`.

## R1: the depth distribution is one band

Mean over 3 seeds, `backbite` 0:

| set | D (max depth) | mean depth | share with depth >= 33 | f0 | blockers per piece |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| square | 714-865 | 216-290 | 77% | 0.49-0.59% | 100-108 |
| tunnels | 941-1109 | 287-374 | 86% | 0.12-0.21% | 92-103 |
| skeleton | 570-762 | 141-230 | 75% | 0.56-0.68% | 83-96 |

At this size the blocking graph is deep and dense: three quarters of all pieces
sit at depth 33 or more, the pieces free at the start are half a percent, and a
ray crosses a hundred pieces on average (up to ~400). A difficulty preset
cannot aim at a *band* of this distribution, because there is effectively one
band. Only the tail is steerable: the 0-2 end (`f0`, the opening moves)
against the 33+ bulk. Any band would have to be relative to the board's own
`D`, because the backbite alone moves `D` by 30-43% (below).

## R1: a ray walk per head is too slow, a line table is not

What `carveOne` would pay to score every candidate head by walking its ray,
sampled every 500th cut:

| set | seed | heads per cut | ray steps per cut | ray walk total | table upkeep | ratio | disagreements |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| square | 1 | 631 (max 4000) | 86 581 | 7.46e9 | 4.0e6 | 1867x | 0 |
| square | 2 | 681 | 86 792 | 7.47e9 | 4.0e6 | 1870x | 0 |
| tunnels | 1 | 628 | 38 906 | 3.32e9 | 4.0e6 | 831x | 0 |
| tunnels | 2 | 639 | 38 134 | 3.26e9 | 4.0e6 | 816x | 0 |

A whole 1000×1000 board takes ~12 s, so 3-7 **billion** extra steps is
several boards' worth of work.

The way out is a fact the engine already guarantees: `headCandidate` returns
the first free cell counting from the exit edge, so **the ray of a head is
exactly the assigned prefix of its line**. A table with one value per line,
`lineMax[d][line]` (the greatest depth on that prefix), answers the score in
O(1), and its upkeep only walks the cells the frontier advances over: 4.0e6
cells per board, four passes over the grid. The script kept that table beside
the ray walk and compared them for every piece: **0 disagreements in 4
boards**, so the cheap read is exact. The engine keeps the same kind of table
for the trap bit, `lineHomo`.

## R2: the tail backbite

### Closure, stalls and lengths at caps 0 and 8

4 sets × 3 seeds × caps 0/2/4/8, 48 boards. **48/48 closed, 0 backtracks, 0
restarts, 0 timeouts**, including the `edge` corner, so no rule coupling
`backbite` to `pStraight` is needed.

This sweep let the bite reach position 0, which reverses the path from
`cells[1]` and so moves the neck, the cell behind the head where `pieceShape`
starts the line. `backbiteTail` now bites at position 1 and up. The lengths
below are 8-10% too generous; the fixed move is measured in the next
section. Mean over 3 seeds:

| skeleton | 8 | 10.5 | 56 862 | 17.6 | 16 456 | 116 486 | 0.823 | 6613 | 314 976 | 357 |
| edge | 0 | 15.6 | 78 370 | 12.8 | 290 | 323 157 | 0.880 | 8290 | 130 770 | 539 |
| edge | 8 | 12.2 | 54 548 | 18.3 | 565 | 178 845 | 0.857 | 8279 | 350 688 | 408 |

Length histogram, share of pieces (mean over 3 seeds):

| set | bb | 2-6 | 7-15 | 16-49 | 50+ | pieces of 50+ cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| square | 0 | 60.4% | 19.7% | 15.9% | 4.0% | 3442 |
| square | 8 | 56.3% | 19.1% | 16.6% | 8.0% | 4751 |

| edge | 8 | 52.4% | 18.9% | 19.1% | 9.6% | 5243 |

1. **Ordered lengths are not honoured more often.** The stall *rate* barely
   moves (0.874 -> 0.848 on square, 0.898 -> 0.896 on tunnels). What drops is
   the wasted work: attempts fall 30-55% and so do stalls, because fewer,
   longer pieces are cut. The growth loop orders ~355 cells per attempt and
   lands 12; with backbite 8 it lands 17. The gap comes from `targetLength`'s
   log-uniform long bucket (16..2500 at this size), which a stall escape
   cannot close.
2. **Long wound pieces without a skeleton: yes.** The longest piece roughly
   doubles (417 -> 793 on square), and the 50+ bucket doubles its share with
   30% fewer pieces overall: 3442 -> 4751 pieces of 50+ cells.
3. **Fewer stubs: mildly.** The 2-6 bucket falls 60.4% -> 56.3%; over half of
   all pieces are still stubs.
4. **The cost is the leftover test.** `strandTrunc` stays at ~7000, but
   `strandLoss` triples: the same number of truncations each throw away a
   longer tail. It costs no wall clock, because the attempts it saves outweigh
   the cells it wastes.
5. **The backbite makes boards shallower.** D falls 30-43% (811 -> 464 on
   square) and mean depth with it (290 -> 128 on square): fewer, longer pieces
   mean a ray crosses fewer of them.

stall:

| set | cap 2 | cap 4 | cap 8 |
|---|---|---|---|

| edge | 21.6% | 33.7% | 49.1% |

pieces mean fewer growth loops to stall in.

sign (tunnels +1.5, -11.9, -4.7 s; edge -14.4, 0.0, -9.6 s).

**It costs `strandLoss`**, which doubles by cap 2 and triples by cap 8. Free
arrows fall by a quarter at cap 2 and then flatten; D falls with the cap
throughout (square 811 -> 516).

## R1 as a trap lever

A piece has exactly one blocker when its line prefix has a single owner. So
the test is one integer per line, kept like the line table above: -1 empty,
-2 several owners, >= 0 the sole owner (`Carver.lineHomo`).

1000×1000, 3 seeds, all closed with 0 backtracks:

| setting | pieces | free arrows | traps | traps as share | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--start=random` | 85 666 | 454 | 680 | 0.79% | 811 |
| `--start=layers` | 85 386 | 549 | 672 | 0.79% | 1046 |
| `--start=tunnels` | 85 506 | 142 | 521 | 0.61% | 1003 |
| rank by most free | 85 828 | 556 | 371 | 0.43% | 1136 |
| **`trapBias` +1** | 85 028 | 276 | **912** | **1.07%** | 1010 |
| **`trapBias` -1** | 85 346 | 395 | **178** | **0.21%** | 1052 |

A 5.1x range on traps, against 1.3x for every other knob. And every other
knob moves free arrows and traps together (layers lifts both, tunnels drops
both, short pieces triple both), while `trapBias` -1 cuts traps to a quarter
and leaves free arrows roughly where they are. No other knob separates the
two, and the separation is what README calls the difficulty: seeing which
arrow is really free.

Ranking heads to make the most free arrows (the "rank by most free" row, not
in the engine) gives 165-556 free arrows, against 142-549 for the `--start`
settings and 320-2417 for the length knobs. Ranking heads cannot beat the
geometry: a piece is free exactly when its head is on the rim, and only the
number of pieces changes how many heads get there.

## R1 with R2

`measure-r1-r2-interaction.ts` (1000×1000, 3 seeds, 13 settings, 39 boards)
and `measure-r1-start-shadow.ts` (200×200, and the two backtracking families
of `absorb.test.ts`). Every one of the 39 boards closed with 0 restarts and 0
backtracks.

### Traps at 1000×1000

Median of 3 seeds, per-seed values in brackets (seeds 1, 2, 3):

| | `backbite 0` | `backbite 8` |
|---|---|---|
| `trapBias -1` | **167** [215, 167, 153] | **107** [98, 107, 195] |
| `trapBias 0` | 695 [628, 695, 716] | 611 [606, 611, 663] |
| `trapBias +1` | **948** [948, 955, 832] | 741 [741, 644, 762] |

The lever alone spans 5.7x on the medians and 4.4-5.7x per seed. The pair
spans 8.9x on the medians, but **the low corner is where the seed decides**:
98, 107 and 195 traps, a 2x spread, against 7% at the default and 13% at +1.

At +1 every seed loses traps to the backbite (-22%, -33%, -8%). At -1 it is
not reproducible: two seeds more than halve and the third rises 27%. On its
own the backbite costs 12% of the traps on the medians, so it does not undo
the lever.

### Free arrows

Same runs, `backbite` 0, median of 3 seeds:

| setting | traps | free arrows | D |
|---|---|---|---|
| `trapBias 0` | 695 | 436 | 853 |
| `trapBias -1` | 167 (-76%) | 403 (-8%) | 1097 (+29%) |
| `trapBias +1` | 948 (+36%) | 273 (-37%) | 1007 (+18%) |

`trapBias` -1 does **not** lift free arrows at the defaults: it leaves them
about where they are while cutting traps fourfold. At the `edge` corner it
does lift them, 277 -> 348 (+26%). `D` rises at both ends, so a board with few
traps is not a board with a shorter blocking chain.

### The `edge` corner

3 seeds each, all closed, 0 backtracks, 0 restarts:

| setting | traps | free arrows | genMs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `trap 0 / bb 0` | 559 | 277 | 14 990 |
| `trap -1 / bb 0` | 130 | 348 | 32 861 |
| `trap -1 / bb 8` | 94 | 220 | 30 088 |
| `trap +1 / bb 0` | 744 | 126 | 35 437 |
| `trap +1 / bb 8` | 604 | 91 | 25 506 |

The corner behaves like the square board and fills at every combination, so
the lever needs no rule in `RULES`.

### The lever and `--start`

`headBias` is read in one place in the carver, and only while `mix` is below
0 (the `startPair` rule). These runs measured a ranking where the trap bit
replaced the depth ranking: with the lever on, `square`, `tunnels` and
`layers` gave **one identical board** at 200×200 (3 seeds, -1 and +1), so
`--start` had no effect. The engine now uses the trap bit as the outer key and
the depth ranking inside each bucket (`carveOne`, `orderByDepth`), so
`--start` reaches the board with the lever on.

### The undo path

Ordinary runs have `backtracks: 0`, so the branch that rebuilds a line after
an undo never runs. With the lever on, the two backtracking families gave **11
runs that undo at least one cut, 61 backtracks in total, and 0 lines** where
the table disagrees with a fold of the finished board (160 lines per 40×40
board, 800 per 200×200). The deepest run, `starved heads` seed 8 at -1, had 50
backtracks and 0 mismatches. That check runs at the end only;
`engine.test.ts` checks the table after every undo.

## What the trap lever costs

Median generation time over the same 39 runs. Two controls isolate the terms:
`tunnels` and the "rank by most free" spike both rank the heads and **neither
keeps a line table**. Two implementations of the lever were timed on the same
seeds:

- a scan of all 2W+2H line headers on every cut, and a sort by the trap bit;
- the engine's: the fold runs in `recomputeLines`, the one place a frontier
  depth changes, with -2 treated as absorbing, and a stable two-bucket
  partition instead of a sort.

**Both give the same boards**: all 39 rows match on pieces, traps, free
arrows, D, longest piece, backtracks and bites, and the recorded
fingerprints are identical. Medians of 3 seeds, normalised against each run's
default board, because the machine drifts ~5% between runs:

| setting | scan and sort | engine | normalised |
|---|---|---|---|
| `trap 0 / bb 0` (default) | 12.3 s | 12.2 s | 1.00x -> 1.00x |
| control `tunnels` | 21.6 s | 22.1 s | 1.75x -> 1.80x |
| control "most free" | 20.6 s | 21.0 s | 1.67x -> 1.71x |
| `trap -1 / bb 0` | 31.4 s | 24.6 s | 2.55x -> 2.01x |
| `trap +1 / bb 0` | 26.2 s | 24.7 s | 2.12x -> 2.02x |

Against the controls, a ranking with no table costs 1.71-1.80x and the lever
costs 2.01x. So **the table's own share is +11%, the same for both signs**
(the scan made it +21% at +1 and +45% at -1).

The rest is the quarter pools, not overhead. A ranked cut draws from the
first quarter of the list and tries the next quarters when it fails, so a
biased ranking goes through more failed attempts than an unbiased one. Every
biased setting pays it, `tunnels` included. The lever costs **the price of a
biased `--start` plus about a tenth for the table**; going below that means
changing the quarter pools, which changes every board.

## The share of ranked cuts is not monotone

A continuous knob would set the share of cuts that rank by the trap bit,
stepping 0.05. `measure-r1-share.ts` sweeps eleven points at 1000×1000, 3
seeds each; every board closed with 0 backtracks and 0 restarts. The
endpoints make no draw, so 0 and ±1 are the boards above.

| trapBias | traps (median) | per seed | free arrows | D | genMs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| -1.0 | **167** | 215, 167, 153 | 403 | 1097 | 25.8 s |
| -0.8 | 479 | 479, 464, 485 | 554 | 1017 | 17.5 s |
| -0.6 | 543 | 520, 543, 608 | 524 | 856 | 16.2 s |
| -0.4 | 630 | 630, 538, 640 | 494 | 858 | 16.0 s |
| -0.2 | 687 | 733, 687, 675 | 517 | 733 | 13.8 s |
| 0 | 695 | 628, 695, 716 | 436 | 853 | 12.0 s |
| +0.2 | 820 | 794, 820, 831 | 511 | 790 | 10.9 s |
| +0.4 | 887 | 887, 887, 835 | 417 | 798 | 14.0 s |
| +0.6 | **995** | 1020, 995, 976 | 387 | 915 | 14.2 s |
| +0.8 | 975 | 922, 975, 980 | 323 | 1036 | 20.3 s |
| +1.0 | 948 | 948, 955, 832 | 273 | 1007 | 25.0 s |

**It is not monotone.** The trap count peaks at +0.6 and falls through +0.8
to +1; per seed the peak is never at +1. The drops sit inside the seed
spread, so from +0.6 up the curve is flat: the last 40% of the positive side
buys nothing.

**A 0.05 step is four times below the noise.** The median step between
neighbouring points is 66 traps and the median seed spread within a point is
58. A 0.2 step is worth about one seed of noise.

**The avoiding side lives at its endpoint.** 0 -> -0.8 covers 41% of the
range to -1, and the last step the other 59%: -0.8 still leaves 479 traps
(31% below 695), -1 leaves 167 (76% below). One cut in five ranked the old
way creates traps nothing later removes.

The interior does one thing the endpoint does not: -0.8 reaches 554 free
arrows against 436 at 0 (+27%), while -1 drops to 403. A board with fewer
traps *and* more free arrows exists at about -0.8.

Generation time tracks the share of ranked cuts: 12 s at 0, 14-17 s in the
middle, 25-26 s at either end.

| huge | 160 000 | 2.12% | 0.42% | 2.63% | 0.20x | 1.24x |
| insane | 1 000 000 | **0.81%** | 0.25% | 1.10% | 0.31x | 1.37x |

**The ladder already exists, and it runs backwards.** The trap share falls
from `easy` to `insane`, a 24x spread, with no knob involved. On the axis
README calls the difficulty, `easy` is the hardest level and `insane` the
easiest: one piece in five looks free on a 25×50 board, one in 123 at the
largest size. On a small board a piece's way out is short and rarely crosses
more than one other piece.

**No assignment of three states makes the share rise with difficulty.**
`avoid` for easy and medium, `off` in the middle and `seek` at the top gives:

| | easy | medium | hard | nightmare | extreme | huge | insane |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `off` everywhere | 19.65% | 9.76% | 8.00% | 5.66% | 3.19% | 2.12% | 0.81% |
| avoid / off / seek | 5.19% | 1.69% | 8.00% | 5.66% | 4.05% | 2.63% | 1.10% |

The first row falls strictly; the second goes down, up, down. The lever spans
about 6x inside a level while the levels span 24x between them. Avoiding is
strong (0.13-0.31x) and seeking weak (1.24-1.61x), so the top of a ladder is
where the lever has least to give.

**Inside a level it is worth having.** With `--start`, the pair spans a level
widely:

| level | lowest | highest | span |
|---|---|---|---|
| easy | 2.19% (`easy-tunnels` avoid) | 36.99% (`easy-square` seek) | 16.9x |
| hard | 0.90% (`hard-tunnels` avoid) | 14.63% (`hard-square` seek) | 16.2x |
| nightmare | 0.28% (`nightmare-tunnels` avoid) | 13.52% (`nightmare-square` seek) | 48.7x |
| insane | 0.19% (`insane-square` avoid) | 1.13% (`insane-skeleton` seek) | 5.8x |

On `hard-square` the lever spans 12.8x where `--start` spans 2.7x. Its value
is a choice made within a level, not a number a level carries.
`huge-400-skeleton` is the one option of 26 where `avoid <= off <= seek`
fails (302 traps at `seek`, 309 at `off`), inside the seed spread.

`easy` being the most trap-dense level is backwards from its name; `avoid`
takes it from 19.65% to 5.19%. That is a question about what "easy" should
mean, which these numbers cannot settle.

## What the knobs became

- **`trapBias`** is three-state: -1/0/1 shown as `avoid / off / seek`
  (`control: choice`), with the words in `WORDS` in `command.ts`. A
  continuous share is not monotone where it matters, its usable step is
  coarser than one seed of noise, and on the avoiding side only the endpoint
  is worth having. `CarverOptions.trapBias` still accepts a share, for
  `measure-r1-share.ts`. No preset sets it.
- **`backbite`** is a `lengths` knob, 0..8 step 1, default 0, in no preset
  and no `--randomized` draw. At 0 the carver makes no extra draw, so boards
  are unchanged. `engine.test.ts` checks the move keeps the head, the neck,
  the cell set and a simple path.
